# Buying a business that depends on one platform: concentration risk beyond customers

By Joash Boyton, CEO of [Acquiry](https://www.acquiry.com/)

Buyers of digital businesses are used to checking customer concentration. Platform concentration deserves the same attention. Many digital businesses depend on a single search engine, app store, marketplace, social network, advertising platform or payment provider for most of their revenue, and that dependency transfers with the business.

## 1. Identify every platform the business relies on

List each platform and what it provides: traffic, distribution, payments, hosting, or a core integration. For each one, estimate the share of revenue that would be affected if the relationship changed. Write it down, because owners often underestimate how concentrated the business is.

## 2. Read the platform's terms, not just the dashboard

Platform terms set out what the business is allowed to do, how accounts can be suspended, and what happens on a change of ownership. Some marketplaces and app stores restrict account transfers or require their approval. Confirm the transfer route before signing, not after.

## 3. Look at the account's own history

Ask for any policy warnings, suspensions, rejected listings or appeals. A business with a clean history on a platform is worth more than one that has been reinstated twice, even if current revenue looks identical.

## 4. Test how exposed the economics are

Platforms change fee structures, commission rates and algorithms. Model what happens to earnings if the take rate rises by a few points or traffic falls by a quarter. If the business only works at today's terms, the price should reflect that.

## 5. Check for a direct relationship with customers

A business that owns its customer list, has repeat buyers and receives direct traffic has somewhere to go if a platform changes. One that has no contact with its customers outside the platform does not. This difference often matters more than the raw revenue figure.

## 6. Plan the transfer in detail

Agree who will hold each account at completion, how access will be handed over, and what happens if a platform refuses a transfer. Where transfer is uncertain, consider holding back part of the price until the account moves successfully.

## 7. Reflect it in the valuation

Platform dependency is a real risk, and it belongs in the multiple rather than in a footnote.
