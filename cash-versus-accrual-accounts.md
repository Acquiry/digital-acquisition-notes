# Cash versus accrual: reading the accounts of a small digital business

*By Joash Boyton, CEO of [Acquiry](https://www.acquiry.com/), Melbourne.*

Many small online businesses keep their books on a cash basis: income is recorded when money arrives and costs when they are paid. That is simple for the owner, but it can distort the picture a buyer needs, especially in subscription and annual billing models.

### Where the distortion appears

- **Annual plans.** Cash accounts show a full year of revenue in the month of payment, inflating that month and flattering growth.
- **Prepaid costs.** Annual software or advertising paid upfront makes one month look expensive and the rest look cheap.
- **Unpaid bills.** Costs incurred but not yet paid are missing, so profit looks higher than it is.

### What buyers do

Buyers usually rebuild the accounts on an accrual basis, spreading revenue over the service period and matching costs to the months they relate to. This produces a more reliable view of monthly earnings and reveals the obligation to serve customers who have already paid. The [free SDE and EBITDA calculator](https://www.acquiry.com/tools/sde-ebitda-calculator/) is a good first step once the figures are on a consistent basis.

### Why it affects price

Valuation multiples are applied to earnings. If the earnings figure is overstated by timing, the price will be too. Comparing the restated figure with [digital business valuation multiples](https://www.acquiry.com/data/multiples/) gives a fairer starting point for negotiation.

### For sellers

Owners planning a sale can save time by preparing accrual-based management accounts for the last two to three years, reconciled to bank statements. It shortens diligence and builds trust.
