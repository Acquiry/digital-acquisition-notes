# Reseller and white-label revenue: what buyers check in an acquisition

Some digital businesses sell most of their product through others: agencies that resell a software tool, partners who badge it as their own, or distributors in other markets. This revenue can be efficient and scalable, but it shifts part of the customer relationship to someone the buyer is not acquiring.

## Who owns the end customer

The first question is whether the business knows who its end customers are. If a reseller holds the relationship, billing and data, the buyer is really acquiring a contract with the reseller, not a customer base. That affects retention risk and how the revenue should be valued.

## Reading the partner agreements
- Term and termination. Can the reseller walk away at short notice?
- Change of control. Does a sale need the partner's consent, or give it a right to terminate?
- Exclusivity and territory. Has the business granted exclusive rights that would limit a buyer's growth plans?
- Pricing and margin. What discount does the partner receive, and can it be renegotiated?

## Concentration and dependency

If one or two partners generate a large share of revenue, buyers will treat that like customer concentration. Ask for revenue by partner for at least two years and look for trends.

## Valuation and structure

Partner revenue may attract a lower multiple than direct recurring revenue, depending on how secure it is.
